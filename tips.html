A LITTLE LOCAL KNOWLEDGE

Travel slowly. Ask first.
Leave room for the unexpected.

The best moments on this route are often the small ones: tea in Patan, a conversation over lunch, or a clear ridge after the clouds lift.

01 / NEPAL

Nepal: courtyards, tea & trail life

Customs that make a difference

  • A simple “Namaste”, with palms together, is a warm greeting. Remove shoes where requested in homes and religious places.
  • Walk clockwise around Buddhist stupas. Offer and receive things with your right hand or both hands, and avoid pointing feet at people or sacred objects.
  • Ask before photographing people or ceremonies. Some Hindu temples exclude non-Hindus; accept the boundary and enjoy permitted courtyards.
  • Keep clothing modest in religious places and villages. Do not share a used spoon with communal food.

Nepal Tourism Board · etiquette ↗

What to make time for

  • In Patan, combine the museum with unstructured walks through courtyards and metalworking lanes. Ask before entering workshops.
  • Try a freshly cooked dal bhat, steamed momos and a Newari meal recommended by your host. In Bhaktapur, seek fresh juju dhau if dairy agrees with you.
  • At Boudhanath, observe the evening circuit respectfully; a quiet tea stop is more rewarding than trying to photograph every prayer wheel.
  • At Pokhara, keep the post-trek evening for a lakeside stroll. Skip a packed activity programme after the long descent.

Nepal Tourism Board · Kathmandu Valley ↗

At a teahouse

  • Expect simple rooms, shared toilets and variable hot water. Ask what costs extra before using showers, charging or Wi-Fi.
  • Eat at the lodge where you sleep unless agreed otherwise; meal income is important to the accommodation business. Order dinner early.
  • Carry toilet paper, hand sanitiser, earplugs, a headtorch and a small dry bag. Pack out litter, including batteries and wipes.
  • Keep noise low in the evenings. Your guide can help settle bills and explain local expectations.

Cash & everyday decisions

  • Agree taxi fares before setting off and keep small NPR notes handy. Ask for official tickets at heritage sites.
  • Ask an artisan about the material, technique and origin before buying. Prefer a small piece you understand over an impulse “antique”.
  • Ask your hotel about festival closures and crowded travel dates during this autumn period; reserve important transport early.
  • Keep a photo of your passport separately from the original, and avoid carrying all your money in one place.

02 / BHUTAN

Bhutan: participate with curiosity

Monasteries & meaningful visits

Cover shoulders and knees at religious sites. Follow your guide on shoes, hats, seating and photography; do not assume indoor photos are allowed. Ask before taking portraits.

For prayers, butter lamps and the blessing ceremony, let the host set the pace. At the Bumdra sky-burial area, follow access instructions and give private religious activity space.

Bhutan Tourism · visitor FAQs ↗

Taste the country

Try ema datshi (chillies and cheese), kewa datshi (potatoes and cheese), red rice, momos and suja, the salty butter tea. Tell your guide your chilli tolerance: request a mild serving rather than assuming “a little spicy” will be mild.

Use the home meal and rural visit to ask about everyday cooking. Accept a small taste of a new dish if you wish, and state allergies clearly.

Bhutan Tourism · food ↗

Experiences already waiting for you

Give the arts-school session, national dress, mandala activity and farmhouse visit time to unfold. Ask how local hosts are paid and buy directly from makers when something speaks to you.

Ask your guide about an archery practice or market visit if it naturally fits. These are optional ideas, not extra items promised in your package.

Seasonal reality & souvenirs

Your Phobjikha stop is 1–2 October: enjoy the wetland landscape and conservation centre, but do not expect the later autumn arrival of wild cranes.

Ask the guide about export paperwork for art or religious objects. Keep receipts. Leave a drone at home unless explicitly authorised in advance; Bhutan generally does not permit tourists to bring one.

03 / HEALTH & CONDITIONS

Looking after yourselves in the mountains

Symptoms change the itinerary

New headache with nausea, dizziness or unusual fatigue after ascending can indicate altitude illness. Tell the guide and do not climb higher to sleep while symptomatic. If symptoms worsen, descend. Breathlessness at rest, confusion or difficulty walking require urgent descent and medical help. CDC · altitude guidance ↗

Pace & previous altitude exposure

Above 3,000 m, CDC guidance recommends no more than about 500 m of sleeping-altitude gain per night, plus extra acclimatisation time after 1,000 m gained. Fitness does not prevent altitude illness.

The Bhutan high night may help, but it does not guarantee you will tolerate Mardi Himal. The Badal Danda stop is intentional. Ask a travel clinician about personal risks and preventive medication; do not improvise treatment on the trail.

CDC · altitude guidance ↗

The ridge has the final say

October can bring clear views, but rain, cloud, cold, wind or snow still change the route. Start early for the best chance of visibility, while letting the guide decide whether darkness, ice or wind makes a start unsafe.

Carry waterproofs even on a blue-sky morning. Use the forecast and local observations, not a calendar promise. Ask about traction only if conditions warrant it and your guide considers it appropriate.

Food, water & animals

Use sealed, boiled or reliably treated water, including for tooth-brushing where needed. Choose thoroughly cooked food served hot; wash or sanitise hands before eating.

Do not pet dogs or monkeys. After any bite or scratch, wash thoroughly and seek prompt medical care. Use mosquito repellent in the warmer valleys as well as sun protection on the ridge.

CDC · Nepal travel health ↗ · CDC · Bhutan travel health ↗

Before you leave / as soon as possible

Review routine vaccinations and destination advice with a travel clinic, especially hepatitis A, typhoid and rabies considerations. Carry prescription medicines in labelled packaging with enough spare supply.

Confirm rescue cover and keep the insurer’s assistance number offline. A helicopter cannot be assumed available in poor weather; your guide’s descent plan matters.

Small details that help every day

Know your clocks

Nepal is UTC+5:45; Bhutan UTC+6; Bangkok UTC+7. During this trip Brussels is UTC+2. Bhutan is only 15 minutes ahead of Nepal, so manually check flight alarms and meeting times after each border crossing.

Tipping without awkwardness

Bhutan guide/driver tips are excluded from the package. Nepal guide and porter tips should also be budgeted separately unless your quote says otherwise. Ask the operator about customary ranges, then choose based on service and your budget; there is no compulsory universal percentage.

Back up the essentials

Save tickets, eVisas, insurance and hotel pins offline on both phones, with a separate secure backup. Share the trek route and expected return date with someone at home. Agree with your guide when a missed check-in should trigger action.

Stay aware of changing conditions

The Belgian advice checked on 25 September flags heightened vigilance and a recent flood/landslide warning around the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli corridor. Have a local operator verify any road fallback; do not assume every road is open. Avoid demonstrations and follow local instructions.

Give the high ridge time.
